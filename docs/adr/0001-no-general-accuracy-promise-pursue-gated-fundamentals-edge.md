# No general FX-accuracy promise; pursue demonstrated edge via fundamentals + tested sentiment, gated by backtest

The app's forecasts don't reliably beat a naive "assume no change" baseline for most currency pairs — verified directly against 19-27 years of this app's own rate history, not just cited from the literature (Meese & Rogoff, 1983, and every major replication since: no general model reliably beats the random walk for major floating currencies at these horizons, out of sample). We will not promise or build toward general "accurate FX direction prediction" — no realistic amount of engineering effort delivers that, and promising it would repeat the exact "confidently wrong" failure mode this app already shipped once (exponential smoothing used unconditionally, never checked against naive).

Instead: pursue *demonstrated, per-pair* edge by extending the existing macro-fundamentals regression (interest-rate differential today; adding inflation, GDP growth, and trade-balance differentials, since the literature ties fundamentals-based outperformance specifically to emerging-market/managed currencies and real-time data) and by testing — not assuming — whether news sentiment has real predictive power. Both are gated by the same backtest-vs-naive discipline as every other forecast candidate: no signal is shown as directional unless it actually earned that in out-of-sample testing.

## Considered Options

- **Agentic/LLM system replacing the statistical pipeline** — rejected as the primary approach: LLMs are a poor fit for precise numeric time-series prediction specifically, and honestly backtesting one is a separate hard problem (a historical origin date's "prediction" can leak the LLM's training-data knowledge of what actually happened afterward). An LLM-as-narrative-companion (summarizing today's real-world context next to the honest statistical range, asserting no number) remains a viable future addition — it plays to what LLMs are actually good at instead of against it.
- **Ship whatever the new signals produce regardless of backtest result** — rejected. The whole point of this decision is refusing to repeat the ungated-model mistake; `no_signal` stays the correct outcome for any pair/horizon where a candidate doesn't demonstrate real edge, no matter how much real-world reasoning went into producing it.
